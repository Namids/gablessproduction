Why Value-Based Ambulatory Care Is Gaining Ground in Africa

5 min read

Most healthcare systems — public and private — are still built around fee-for-service: a provider is paid for each visit, test or procedure performed, regardless of whether the patient actually gets healthier. Value-based care flips that. Providers are paid based on patient outcomes, not patient volume.

It's a model that started gaining serious traction in the US and Europe over the past decade. What's newer — and, we'd argue, more interesting — is how well it fits the specific pressures facing ambulatory (outpatient) care across Africa right now.

Why ambulatory care specifically

Ambulatory care covers everything from routine checkups to chronic disease management delivered outside a hospital admission — diabetes, hypertension, maternal health, follow-up cardiology care. These are exactly the conditions where consistent, ongoing management prevents far more expensive hospital admissions later. A payment model that rewards keeping a diabetic patient stable is a very different incentive from one that only pays when that patient is admitted with complications.

Three pressures pushing institutions toward it

Resource constraints. Many hospitals in the region are managing patient loads that outpace hospital bed capacity. Value-based ambulatory models shift appropriate care out of expensive inpatient settings and into outpatient clinics designed to manage it well — freeing hospital capacity for the cases that genuinely need it.

Chronic disease growth. Non-communicable diseases — diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease — are rising sharply across the continent. These are chronic-management conditions by nature, and chronic management is where value-based, outcomes-focused care shows its biggest advantage over episodic, fee-for-service visits.

Donor and insurer expectations. International health financing bodies and increasingly sophisticated private insurers are asking harder questions about outcomes, not just service counts. Institutions that can already report on and demonstrate outcomes are simply better positioned for that funding conversation.

"Value-based care isn't a policy trend to wait out. For ambulatory care in particular, it's closer to where the funding and the patient need are both already heading."

What it takes to actually make the shift

Moving to value-based ambulatory care isn't a billing change you can make overnight. It requires reliable patient data to measure outcomes in the first place, clinical workflows built around ongoing management rather than single visits, and staff trained to work within that model. This is exactly the kind of transition where operational and technology consulting makes the difference between an ambitious announcement and a model that actually holds up a year in.

The institutions we've seen make this shift successfully didn't try to convert their entire outpatient operation at once. They picked one high-impact chronic condition, proved the model worked, and expanded from there.
